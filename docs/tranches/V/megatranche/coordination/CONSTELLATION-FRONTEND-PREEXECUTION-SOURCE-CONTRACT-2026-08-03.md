## Why this layer is necessary

The current source packets fail in recurrent, cross-repository ways:

- raw subject identifiers collide across repositories;
- direct imports are pinned while runtime-consumed styles, wrappers, route
  parents, or transitive consumers are omitted;
- dynamic `:is`, Teleport/portal, duplicate mount, and activation predicates
  disappear behind family-level summaries;
- a theoretical Cartesian product silently invents applicability or
  `N/A` instead of proving it from source;
- a family template is copied into many D1 wrappers and called
  subject-specific design;
- D2 is emitted by the D1 author or shares its answer key;
- a page screenshot is allowed to stand in for child component and state
  evidence;
- caller-supplied summaries and field-presence validators remain green when
  production-consumed semantics are deleted.

The contract makes each of those conditions fatal before any real-platform
execution can be authorized.

## Authenticated packet

A repository owner must create a fresh, absent, immutable packet root. It
contains exactly the 17 members named by the machine declaration: packet and
source manifests; subjects; surface contracts; topology; applicability; D1;
D2; author lineage; independent oracles; Kronecker plan; platform profiles;
performance budgets; zero-credit receipt; validator; raw validator-process
receipt; and checksums.

Every packet member is a regular file. Symlinks, specials, unlisted files,
unused pins, caller summaries, filesystem rereads after snapshot
authentication, and post-write root ambiguity are fatal. The packet binds the
pre-write absence receipt plus exact before/after tree identities.

Canonical identifiers use UTF-8, Unicode codepoint key order, declared array
order, and versioned length-framed components. The exact identifier is
`SHA-256(UTF8(domain) || NUL || U64BE(length) || UTF8(component) ...)`, with
one length/value frame per component and no Unicode normalization. No
host-locale ordering or raw, unnamespaced subject identifier is admissible.

## Source closure

`SOURCE-MEMBERS.json` is a closed byte registry. Every route, mount, export,
component, style, copy source, asset, API client, storage client,
authorization gate, fixture, and governing receipt consumed by a claim is
present with repository identity, path, bytes, mode, nlink, SHA-256, and
semantic role.

Every source span named elsewhere resolves to this registry. Transitive
runtime-consumed source is mandatory; direct imports alone do not close a
subject. Missing source, an unresolved lazy target, or a silent `continue` is
fatal.

## Subjects and topology

A subject is a page, a mounted instance, an unmounted public export with a
real future harness, an Admin/debug/error view, or an embedded visual runtime.
Its identity is the length-framed tuple:

`repository + kind + canonical route/harness + canonical mount/export path + instance ordinal`.

Duplicate mounts remain distinct instances. Public exports with no product
mount remain audit subjects through explicit harnesses. Unreachable internal
components carry an exact disposition rather than disappearing. Page-level
evidence cannot close child component evidence.

Each subject also has a source-derived surface contract. It records title/H1
law, product role, props, events, slots, variants, defaults, direct and
transitive consumers, mount state, functional and interaction states, copy,
accessibility, and Admin/debug/error applicability. Empty arrays are explicit;
missing fields are not. Active, inactive, idle, interacting, loading, empty,
error, offline, and permission-denied states are each present or carry an
authenticated `N/A` through the applicability registry.

Topology records every route mount, component mount, dynamic candidate,
Teleport/portal, async boundary, authorization gate, API/storage/style
dependency, and error fallback. Each edge binds source spans, activation
predicate, route state, and focus, scroll, storage, API, and cleanup owners.
Dynamic component candidate sets must be closed. Teleports and portals bind
both source instance and target, the open predicate, stacking/focus policy,
and duplicate ordinals.

## Applicability and equivalence

For every subject and every member of axes `R,O,P,C,D,V,L,I,X,N,A,T`, one of
three dispositions is explicit:

1. `REQUIRED`;
2. `NOT_APPLICABLE_AUTHENTICATED` with source-causal witness;
3. `OPEN_RED`.

There is no implicit omission. A generated Cartesian matrix cannot decide
applicability. An `N/A` without exact member IDs and source spans is fatal.

Equivalence is a future execution optimization, never pre-banked evidence. An
equivalence group names its cells, exact equal axes, source reason, and future
witness. It cannot allow one page screenshot to close multiple child
components or active/inactive/idle/error states.

## D1: subject-specific design brief

Every subject receives its own D1 record. Shared tokens and family doctrine
may be referenced, but they cannot replace subject semantics. D1 must name:

- audience, single job, and visual/interactive protagonist;
- information hierarchy and exact user-facing vocabulary;
- display, body, and utility type roles;
- palette, measured chroma, spacing, layout, and container ownership;
- responsive, safe-area, touch-target, focus, VoiceOver, Dynamic Type, and RTL
  law;
- one semantic motion owner with 0 ms, 120 ms, 420 ms, settled, and
  reduced-motion states;
- predeclared performance budgets;
- an explicit shared-design-system reference plus the subject grounding that
  makes this brief more than a family template;
- one memorable detail grounded in the subject rather than generic cards,
  pills, gradients, or decoration;
- applicability of Golden Glass, Breath of Life, and Movement of Momentum.

Template prose copied over distinct subjects is not D1 closure. Identical
semantic cores across different subjects require an authenticated design
equivalence plus a real subject-grounding override; changing only a title or
subject ID is insufficient.

## D2: independent critique

Every subject receives a D2 critique from a different author and a different
root. D2 exact-binds the D1 record and authenticated source but shares no
answer key. It independently evaluates generic defaults, hierarchy, copy,
motion, mobile occlusion, keyboard/focus, accessibility, performance,
removable detail, and the clarity of active, inactive, idle, loading, empty,
error, offline, and permission-denied states.

Each finding ends in `KEEP`, `FOLD`, `MOVE`, `SPLIT`, or `PRUNE` with an exact
owner and receiver. Same-author critique is archaeology, not admissible D2.

`AUTHOR-LINEAGE.json` prevents independence from being asserted by two caller
strings. D1 and D2 must differ by author, task/review identity, and immutable
root; each lineage binds checksums, source snapshot, pre-write absence, raw
process receipt, and before/after tree identities. A task ID alone is
chronology, not evidence.

`ORACLES.json` separately binds functional, copy, accessibility, motion,
geometry, performance, and residue truth to authenticated source and an
author lineage. The candidate generator cannot author its only oracle, and a
caller-expected failure code cannot determine the verdict.

## Kronecker and state contract

Every cell ID is reproduced from the subject ID and all 12 axis selections.
The cell binds applicability records, pre-state, trigger, post-state,
rollback, focus, scroll, storage, API, functional, copy, accessibility,
motion, visual, UX, oracle, performance, and optional equivalence ownership.
At this stage every cell is `NOT_EXECUTED`.

Every required tuple has one cell; every authenticated `N/A` has none; every
`OPEN_RED` remains visible. A missing, duplicate, reordered, or
preimage-inconsistent cell is fatal.

## Performance contract

Budgets precede observation and are subject/interaction specific. They name
metric, threshold, unit, measurement window, device and network class, and
source rationale. At minimum they cover subject-ready/LCP, interaction
latency, long tasks, layout shift, animation frame stability, and resource or
memory growth. A threshold selected after observing the run is invalid.

## Platform profiles

The packet defines both later execution classes: real iOS Simulator Mobile
Safari and the installed desktop Safari. A profile names OS/Safari constraints,
device or host class, viewport, scale, orientation, input modality, color and
contrast preferences, reduced motion, Dynamic Type/text scale, locale,
direction, network, and fresh-state law. At source stage the runtime binding is
explicitly pending. A later raw receipt must exact-bind the installed runtime
and device identity. Chromium, generic emulation, Playwright WebKit, or a
resized desktop window cannot satisfy either Safari class.

## Fatal validation order

The validator derives everything from one authenticated immutable snapshot
and stops on the first applicable class in this order:

1. packet and governing pins;
2. exact source membership and bytes;
3. subject namespace, order, and uniqueness;
4. topology and causal joins;
5. applicability and `N/A` witnesses;
6. subject-specific D1;
7. independent D2 provenance and content;
8. Kronecker preimage and equivalence;
9. state, copy, accessibility, motion, and performance;
10. zero-credit and owner stop.

Later findings are secondary only. A hostile may not claim a desired semantic
falsifier when an earlier authentication or mechanics gate would fail first.

## Control and admission boundary

This source contract specifies future control integrity but executes none. A
later packet validator must consume owner mutations through the same
production path, remove the owned finding only for the owner suppression,
retain it for every non-owner suppression, reject unknown/duplicate/reordered
suppression, and retain raw process receipts. Caller-expected failure codes do
not determine truth.

Passing this contract would accept only a source packet. It would not populate
an owner slot. The four native slots remain `0/4`; the parser receiver remains
separate at `0/1 PAUSED`; strict root policy remains `0/5`. A native owner slot
still needs the distinct 12-field receipt, immutable candidate, production
hostiles, seal, two clean reviews, owner acceptance, and exact before/after
tree identities.
