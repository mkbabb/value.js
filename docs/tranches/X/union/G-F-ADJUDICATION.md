## §4 THE METHOD FINDING — why R-1 escaped four passes

The X·V loop retired one predicate for exactly this disease. §1.L clause 2 struck X-W7-FOLD §R1's
30-character proximity heuristic because *"rows routed by bare declarative phrasing are invisible
to it and were the entire BLOCKER"* — the pass-2 census defect. §1.G clause 7 then **elected** the
row predicate `^\s*([-*]|\|)\s*\*\*` and lifted clause 7's moratorium.

**The elected row predicate has the same shape of blind spot, one axis over.** It admits only
**bold-leading** rows. Records that key their disposition tables with **bare** ids —
`| AP-17 (reader-2 missed #2) | MAJOR | … NO-WAVE-OWNER …` — are invisible to it, wholly, silently,
and by construction. Passes 1–4 each ran a census under the elected predicate; `PASS-4-CHECK-
RETURN.json` returns **CONFORMANT** with three MINOR-with-mitigation defects, all concerning
arithmetic staleness and anchor drift. **None is R-1**, and none could have been: the rows are
outside the predicate that every round measured with.

This is not a charge against the loop's diligence — it is the loop's own law arriving at its own
next instance. §1.L clause 6 already legislates the cure: *"Changing the predicate **obliges**
re-deriving every figure downstream of it."* The corollary this seat adds, and offers as the
finding rather than the defect:

> **THE PREDICATE-COMPLEMENT RULE (proposed, for the union pass to adopt or decline).** A census
> that **elects** a row predicate must, once, measure its **complement** — the rows the predicate
> excludes — and state the complement's size and disposition. An elected predicate with an
> unmeasured complement is not a settled predicate; it is an unfalsifiable one. The complement
> here is **19 rows**, of which **9** were escapes and **7** were real. Measuring it cost one
> regex.
