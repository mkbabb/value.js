### Where it came from

Adobe defined it in 1998 for Photoshop, as a working space large enough for what commercial presses could print, their cyans and greens above all. Photographers have kept it as the step up from sRGB for print work ever since.

### How to read it

sRGB's red and blue primaries, a greener green, the D65 white, and a single power curve with an exponent of 563/256 (about 2.2) and no straight segment at the dark end.

### What to watch for

- An Adobe RGB picture read as if it were sRGB looks dull, because its numbers land on smaller primaries.
- For screens, Display P3 is the closer match; Adobe RGB's extra reach is mostly in green and cyan, P3's in red.
